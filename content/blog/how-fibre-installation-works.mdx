Getting connected to fibre broadband involves more than simply running a cable into your home. Behind every successful installation is a series of technical checks, network connections and equipment configurations that ensure you receive a reliable internet connection.

From checking network availability to installing your equipment and activating your service, here's what happens during a typical fibre installation.

## 1. Network availability and site survey

Before installation begins, your internet service provider (ISP) needs to establish whether fibre infrastructure is available at your location.

This involves checking the existing fibre network, identifying a suitable distribution point and confirming that the network has the capacity to accommodate a new connection.

Depending on the property and existing infrastructure, a physical site survey may be required.

During the assessment, the installation team considers:

- **Network availability:** Whether the property can be connected to the existing fibre network.
- **Connection route:** The most practical route from the nearest suitable fibre distribution point to your premises.
- **Property access:** Whether existing ducts, poles, conduits or building pathways can be used.
- **Equipment placement:** Where the fibre termination point and indoor equipment should be installed.
- **Permissions:** Whether landlord, property management or other necessary approvals are required.

For apartment buildings and commercial premises, additional planning may be necessary to access shared communication ducts, risers and equipment rooms.

The objective is to establish a suitable installation route before any physical work begins.

## 2. Planning and preparing the installation

Once the connection route has been assessed, the installation is planned.

The technician determines the materials, equipment and installation method required for the property.

Fibre can reach a building through different routes depending on the existing network infrastructure.

The two most common methods are:

- **Aerial installation:** Fibre is delivered using overhead cables supported by utility poles or an existing approved aerial route.
- **Underground installation:** Fibre is delivered through underground ducts, conduits or buried cable routes.

The chosen method depends on network design, infrastructure availability, property layout and local installation requirements.

The technician also discusses the cable entry point and the proposed location of the equipment with the customer before proceeding.

## 3. Running the fibre drop cable

This is where the physical connection between the distribution network and your property is established.

The final section of cable connecting a customer's premises to the nearby fibre distribution network is commonly called the *fibre drop cable*.

Depending on the installation design, this cable may be:

- Suspended along an approved overhead route.
- Pulled through an existing underground duct.
- Installed through an approved underground cable route.
- Routed through an apartment building's existing fibre infrastructure.

The drop cable connects to the designated fibre distribution point, which may include a distribution terminal or another connection facility within the network.

In some installations, the cable connects through an external termination or junction box mounted on the building.

The technician ensures the cable is properly supported and protected against environmental conditions and physical damage.

Care is also taken to avoid excessive tension, sharp bends and other conditions that could affect the fibre's performance.

## 4. Bringing the fibre into the building

After the external cable route is established, the fibre is brought into the building.

The entry point is agreed upon with the customer and selected to minimise unnecessary disruption to the property.

Where necessary, a small opening is made through an external wall to accommodate the incoming fibre cable. The entry is then properly sealed to protect against moisture and other environmental elements.

The cable is routed to the agreed equipment location, taking into consideration accessibility, available power and the customer's preferred equipment arrangement.

For properties with existing fibre-ready infrastructure, some of this internal routing may already be in place.

## 5. Fibre termination and ONT installation

Once the fibre reaches the installation point, it must be connected to the appropriate customer-premises equipment.

This involves preparing and connecting the fibre using the termination method specified by the network design. Depending on the system, this may involve factory-terminated connectors or fibre splicing.

The technician then installs an **Optical Network Terminal (ONT)**.

An ONT is an electronic device that receives the optical signal from the fibre network and converts it into an electrical Ethernet connection that can be used by your networking equipment.

In a typical home installation, the equipment includes:

- **Fibre termination point:** The location where the incoming fibre cable is presented or connected.
- **ONT:** Converts the optical connection into an electronic network interface.
- **Wi-Fi router:** Distributes the internet connection to your phones, computers, smart TVs and other devices.

Some modern devices combine the ONT and router into one unit, depending on the ISP's equipment.

The ONT should be installed in an appropriate location with access to electrical power. Its positioning should also allow convenient connection to the router.

## 6. Optical signal testing and connection verification

Before activating the service, the technician needs to establish that the physical fibre connection is functioning correctly.

This is an important stage because a properly installed cable does not automatically guarantee a working broadband connection.

The technician performs the appropriate optical and connectivity checks.

These may include:

- Checking fibre continuity to confirm that the optical path is connected correctly.
- Measuring optical signal levels to ensure they fall within the network equipment's permitted operating range.
- Checking connectors and terminations for potential connection problems.
- Verifying that the ONT can establish communication with the provider's access network.
- Performing additional fault-location or loss testing where necessary.

Specialised optical testing equipment, such as optical power meters and optical loss test sets, is used where appropriate. An Optical Time-Domain Reflectometer (OTDR) may also be used for certain network sections or fault investigations.

Once the physical connection passes the required checks, the installation can proceed to service activation.

## 7. Network configuration and service activation

With the physical connection established, the ISP configures the customer's service on its network.

In a typical fibre-to-the-home network, this involves registering or provisioning the ONT and associating it with the appropriate customer service.

The network configuration may include:

- Assigning the correct customer connection and service profile.
- Configuring the broadband package and applicable speed limits.
- Establishing the required WAN connection.
- Configuring the router according to the ISP's network requirements.
- Confirming that the connection can reach the internet.

The exact configuration depends on the ISP's infrastructure and technology.

Once the required settings are applied, the customer's connection is brought online.

## 8. Router configuration and Wi-Fi setup

After the fibre connection is activated, the technician configures the customer's router.

This includes establishing the connection between the ONT and router, setting up the wireless network and ensuring that customer devices can connect.

The configuration typically covers:

- Setting up the Wi-Fi network name (SSID).
- Configuring a secure Wi-Fi password.
- Selecting appropriate wireless settings.
- Establishing the router's internet connection.
- Checking Ethernet connectivity where applicable.

Router placement is also important.

For better wireless coverage, the router should ideally be positioned in a reasonably central, open location, away from unnecessary obstructions and sources of interference.

Larger properties may require additional access points or a mesh Wi-Fi system to provide consistent coverage throughout the building.

## 9. Final testing and handover

The installation is not complete until the service has been verified.

The technician performs final checks to confirm that the connection is operational and that the customer's equipment is communicating correctly.

This may involve:

- Confirming the ONT's operational status.
- Verifying that the router has an active internet connection.
- Testing connectivity using a connected device.
- Performing a speed test, preferably through a wired Ethernet connection.
- Checking basic Wi-Fi connectivity.
- Confirming that the installed service corresponds to the customer's subscribed package.

A wired speed test is particularly useful because wireless performance can vary depending on the device, distance, walls and radio interference.

Before leaving, the technician should explain the basic equipment indicators, demonstrate that the connection is working and ensure the installation area is left tidy.

The customer can then begin using their new fibre broadband connection.

## How long does fibre installation take?

The installation time depends on the property's location, the availability of existing infrastructure and the complexity of the cable route.

Where the fibre network is already available and the installation route is straightforward, the work may be completed during one appointment.

However, additional work may be necessary when:

- Underground ducts are blocked or unavailable.
- New external cabling or civil construction is required.
- The property needs additional building infrastructure.
- Installation permissions have not yet been obtained.
- The nearest distribution point needs additional network work.

In these situations, the ISP may arrange additional work before completing the customer installation.

## How can you prepare for your fibre installation?

A little preparation can make the installation process smoother.

Before the technician arrives:

1. Ensure there is access to the property and the agreed cable route.
2. Obtain any necessary landlord or building management permissions.
3. Identify a suitable location for the ONT and router.
4. Ensure there is an available electrical socket near the equipment location.
5. Keep the installation area accessible.
6. Discuss any concerns about drilling, cable routing or equipment placement with the technician.

## Ready to get connected?

Fibre installation is a carefully coordinated process that brings together network infrastructure, optical technology and customer-premises equipment.

From the first network assessment to the final connection test, every stage contributes to delivering a reliable broadband experience.

Once everything is installed, tested and activated, you're ready to enjoy the benefits of fibre connectivity.
