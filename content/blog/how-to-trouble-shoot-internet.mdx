Even with a high-speed fibre broadband connection, wireless performance can occasionally degrade...

From basic reboots to channel optimization, here's what you can do to troubleshoot and improve your home or office Wi-Fi network.

## 1. Perform a basic router restart

Many temporary connection issues, memory leaks, or sluggish responses can be resolved with a simple system reboot.

* Unplug the power cable from both your Optical Network Terminal (ONT) and your Wi-Fi router.
* Wait for approximately 30 to 60 seconds to allow internal caches to clear completely.
* Plug the ONT in first and wait for the indicator lights to stabilize.
* Plug your router back in and allow a couple of minutes for the network broadcast to re-establish.

## 2. Evaluate router placement and physical obstructions

Wireless signals rely on radio frequencies that can be weakened or blocked by physical barriers in your environment.

* **Central location:** Position your router in a central, elevated location rather than hidden inside a cabinet or on the floor.
* **Obstructions:** Keep the router away from thick concrete walls, large metal appliances, mirrors, and dense masonry that can absorb or deflect signals.
* **Interference sources:** Avoid placing the router near cordless phones, baby monitors, or microwave ovens operating on overlapping frequencies.

## 3. Test with a wired connection

To determine whether the issue is related to wireless performance or your incoming internet service, perform a diagnostic check using an Ethernet cable.

* Connect a laptop or computer directly to one of the router’s LAN ports using a reliable Ethernet cable.
* Run a speed test to verify if you are receiving your subscribed bandwidth package.
* If the wired connection is fast and stable, the issue is likely isolated to your wireless environment. If the wired connection is also slow, contact your Internet Service Provider (ISP).

## 4. Check for channel congestion and interference

If you live in a densely populated building or close to neighboring offices, nearby Wi-Fi networks might be broadcasting on the same wireless channel, causing congestion.

* Access your router's administrative dashboard using a web browser.
* Navigate to the wireless settings page and check your Wi-Fi channels.
* Switch your 2.4 GHz band to a less congested channel (typically 1, 6, or 11) or migrate compatible devices to the less congested 5 GHz frequency band for better throughput.

## 5. Update router firmware and device drivers

Outdated firmware can cause security vulnerabilities, connection drops, and suboptimal performance.

* Log into your router's management portal and check for available firmware updates provided by the manufacturer.
* Ensure that the network adapter drivers on your computers, smartphones, and smart TVs are up to date.

## 6. Consider network expansion for larger spaces

A single router often struggles to provide uniform coverage across multi-room homes or expansive commercial properties.

* If certain rooms suffer from weak signals ("dead zones"), consider installing a mesh Wi-Fi system or a compatible wireless repeater.
* Mesh systems create a seamless, unified network footprint, ensuring consistent speeds as you move throughout the building.

## How can you prevent future Wi-Fi issues?

Proactive maintenance can keep your network running smoothly over the long term:

1. Restart your networking equipment periodically to clear temporary cache.
2. Keep router firmware updated to benefit from security and stability patches.
3. Periodically check connected devices to ensure no unauthorized users are accessing your network.
4. Monitor network speeds during peak hours to catch performance drops early.

## Ready to optimize your network?

Wi-Fi troubleshooting is a straightforward process when approached methodically. From identifying physical interference to adjusting router channels, every step contributes to a more reliable browsing and streaming experience.

Once you apply these adjustments, you'll be back to enjoying fast, seamless connectivity across all your devices.
